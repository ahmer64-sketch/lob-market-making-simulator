# Phase 1: matching engine

## Correctness

Test suite: **16 passed in 18.22s** (`pytest`):

- **Unit tests** for every rule: price priority, FIFO within a price, partial fills keeping their place in the queue, market orders sweeping levels, cancels (O(1), idempotent, after a fill), post-only rejection, immediate-or-cancel, integer-tick prices, imbalance and microprice.
- **Against a reference engine:** a deliberately naive book (one flat list, re-sorted on every match) must produce identical trades and identical books on (a) 400 order sequences generated by `hypothesis`, (b) 100,000 random events with a shallow, fast-trading book, and (c) 30,000 events building a deep book of over 1,000 resting orders across more than 200 price levels.
- **Invariants after every event:** best bid < best ask (never crossed); every level's total equals the sum of its orders; orders inside a level are in arrival order; and quantity is conserved: everything submitted is filled, resting, cancelled or discarded.
- **The tests were themselves tested** by planting two bugs (serving the newest order first; printing trades at the taker's price instead of the resting order's): both were caught immediately.

## Speed

300,000 events per regime, pure Python 3.13.2 on arm64, best of 3 runs:

| Regime | Events / second | Trades | Cancels that removed an order | Resting orders at end | Price levels at end |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| narrow price range, 15% market orders | **643,963** | 132,919 | 37% | 41,550 | 24 |
| wide price range, 5% market orders | **539,251** | 134,854 | 56% | 70,872 | 231 |

This flow is a stress test, not a realistic market: more quantity arrives as limit orders than market orders and cancels remove, so the book grows to tens of thousands of orders, and only 37–56% of cancels hit an order still resting (the rest target orders that already traded). Realistic, balanced order flow is built in Phase 2.

Design: integer ticks; per price level an insertion-ordered dict (FIFO, O(1) cancel); a heap per side for the best price with lazy removal of emptied levels; an order-id map for O(1) lookup. Faster options for later (Numba, array-based levels, C++/Rust) are discussed in the final README.
